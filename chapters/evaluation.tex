\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:eval}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This chapter evaluates the three subsystems introduced in
Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}: the frame-level click-intent classifier, the
classification-to-segmentation pipeline, and the firmware threshold optimizer. For
each subsystem we define the evaluation protocol, report quantitative results on a
held-out test set, and compare against appropriate baselines.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Dataset and Splits}
All experiments use the curated corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:data_acquisition}:
132 annotated recordings from 39 participants, yielding approximately 12,556,000 frames
at 1\,kHz.  Separate models are trained for the left (L) and right (R) button channels,
reflecting the large asymmetry in click volume (39,088 left-button cycles versus 6,645
right-button cycles).  For each channel the data is split at the participant level into
training (10,794,087 frames, L channel), validation (789,538 frames), and held-out test
(972,663 frames) partitions.  No participant's recordings appear in more than one
partition.  Generalization is assessed with 5-fold grouped cross-validation, where each
fold holds out a distinct group of participants (leave-one-participant-group-out), on
the training and validation pool; the test set is evaluated exactly once per model
after all hyperparameter selection is complete.

\subsection{Baselines}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Majority-class baseline.} Assigns every frame the Background label.
    Provides the floor under severe class imbalance.
  \item \textbf{Heuristic bootstrap.} The OS-event-anchored walk-back detector described
    in Section~\ref{sec:labeling}, evaluated as a segmentor on the test set.
    Represents the pre-model ceiling achievable without a learned component.
  \item \textbf{Static default firmware configuration.} Actuation Point at level 5/10
    (mid-travel), Rapid Trigger disabled. Serves as the optimizer comparison point.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}
\paragraph{Detection and segmentation.}
A predicted click interval is counted as a true positive (TP) when it overlaps a
ground-truth interval by at least 50\,\% of the longer interval (the same
overlap-constrained monotonic matching used by the optimizer).  \emph{Deployment F1}
is the harmonic mean of click-level precision and recall.  \emph{Mean IoU} is the
average Jaccard index over matched pairs.

\paragraph{Boundary timing.}
For each matched pair we compute the signed make-boundary error $e^{(m)}$ and
break-boundary error $e^{(b)}$ in milliseconds (positive = late, negative = early).
We report the mean bias and the absolute spread (mean absolute deviation) per boundary.

\paragraph{Cross-validation.}
For all three architectures (Random Forest, GBT, and FCN), 5-fold grouped
cross-validation deployment F1 is reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation, in
addition to single held-out test set performance.

\paragraph{Hardware.}
All timings are measured on an AMD Ryzen~9 7900X (12-core, 4.7\,GHz), 16\,GB RAM,
no GPU.  The FCN uses PyTorch CPU inference for fair comparison; a GPU would
substantially accelerate training.

\subsection{Pairing Strategy Comparison}
The three pairing strategies (greedy first-next, Hungarian assignment, and trained
logistic scorer) produce nearly identical segmentation IoU across all models and
channels, with a maximum gap of 0.001 IoU points. Once the classifier yields
well-calibrated probability outputs the assignment problem is largely trivial, and
greedy first-next is selected as the default for RF and FCN~L, while the trained
scorer is selected for GBT and Hungarian for FCN~R.

\section{Optimizer Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval-opt}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Comparison with Static Default}
The firmware threshold optimizer is evaluated by comparing the per-user optimal
(AP, RT) configuration against the device's static default (AP\,=\,5/10, RT disabled)
across all 238 channel-records from 132 sessions and 39 participants --- the full
annotated corpus, not limited to the held-out test set. The optimizer is not trained
on any data split; it exhaustively evaluates the (AP, RT) grid for each session by
replaying the recorded analog signal through the firmware FSM simulator, so evaluating
on the full corpus does not leak test-set information used to select model
hyperparameters. For each session
and channel, the optimizer exhaustively searches the 10-level (AP, RT) grid using the
RF model's segmented click intervals as labeled intent, applying the production objective
function ($w_\text{make}=0.6$, $c_\text{det}=40$\,ms, $\beta=0$, $\kappa=2.0$).  The
static default is scored through the identical firmware FSM simulator to ensure a fair
comparison.

\begin{table*}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{Optimizer comparison: static default versus per-user optimized (AP, RT)
configuration. Mean and median over 238 channel-records (132 sessions, 39 participants).}
\label{tab:optimizer}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\hline
\textbf{Metric}

\hline

Table~\ref{tab:optimizer} summarises the comparison. The optimizer reduces loss by
49\,\% (mean) and increases the TP rate from 90.9\,\%
to 97.5\,\%, recovering an additional 6.6 percentage points of labeled click intent.
The improvement is consistent across all 238 channel-records: a Wilcoxon signed-rank
test on the per-session loss delta yields $W=0$ ($n=238$, $p \ll 0.001$), confirming
that the optimized configuration never performs worse than the static default on any
session in the corpus.
The most practically significant result is the make-latency reduction: the static
mid-travel AP (level~5 of 10) requires the user to press well beyond the biomechanical
onset before the firmware registers the event, producing a mean make latency of
27.0\,ms.  The per-user optimized AP reduces this to 15.4\,ms --- an 11.6\,ms
improvement that spans roughly one perceptual just-noticeable difference for
actuation timing ($\approx$8--12\,ms) and represents a directly perceptible
responsiveness gain.

Break latency improves from a mean of 29.1\,ms to 11.2\,ms and a median of 21.5\,ms to
9.0\,ms, with no degenerate RT selections observed: setting the detection-cost
parameter to $c_\text{det}=40$\,ms removes the optimizer's incentive to trade
break-timing accuracy for a marginal TP-rate gain by maxing out RT hysteresis, so
break latency is penalized consistently across all 132 sessions.

Context probes contribute $+2.17\%$ deployment F1, roughly three times the combined
gain of all kinematic derivative channels ($+0.68\%$).  The 10 raw depth samples at
$\pm$10\,ms give the RF direct access to the local depth trajectory, allowing it to
approximate velocity and acceleration implicitly from adjacent probe values; explicit
kinematic channels are therefore largely redundant, and jerk adds a negligible
$+0.04\%$ on top.

Figure~\ref{fig:feat-importance} shows how importance is distributed across channel
groups at each ablation step.  \texttt{analog\_L} --- which subsumes both window
statistics and probe values --- is the dominant group in every configuration.  When
kinematic channels are absent its share reaches 70.6\,\% (with probes) or 81.9\,\%
(window statistics only).  As kinematics are added, velocity absorbs part of that
share but \texttt{analog\_L} never falls below 31\,\%, confirming that the raw
depth trajectory carries information that derived channels cannot fully substitute.
\texttt{digital\_L} remains stable at 18--29\,\% throughout, reflecting the
independent predictive value of the OS-reported press event.

For the RF architecture, probe range and density therefore matter more than channel
engineering.  The natural next ablation is a \emph{wider-probe} study (e.g.\
$\pm$50\,ms or $\pm$100\,ms offsets) to determine whether extending the 100\,ms
probe horizon further narrows the gap to the FCN's 3\,s receptive field.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:eval-discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Hypothesis confirmation.}
The central hypothesis of this thesis is that per-user variation in analog click
telemetry is structured enough to support automatic labeling, statistical modeling,
and parameter optimization beyond static default settings.  The results across all
three subsystems confirm this.  The learned classifier recovers 97.0\,\% of intended
left-channel click events --- a $2.5\times$ improvement over a rule-based heuristic
that cannot detect firmware-unregistered presses.  The optimizer reduces make latency
by 11.6\,ms (median 11.4\,ms) across every one of 238 channel-records, with a
Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-session loss delta ($W=0$) confirming the
improvement is consistent, not an artifact of a few large gains, and that the
structure in individual telemetry is not only learnable but practically exploitable.

\paragraph{Why the RF outperforms the FCN despite its simpler architecture.}
The dilated residual FCN was expected to outperform the Random Forest by exploiting
long-range temporal context (3,060-frame receptive field vs.\ a 33-frame sliding
window).  On the left channel the FCN falls 3.4 percentage points short of the RF
(F1 0.936 vs.\ 0.970) on the single held-out test set; more critically, its
grouped cross-validation collapses to $0.597 \pm 0.174$, with one fold reaching
0.367.  On the right channel the single-test gap is 8.2 points
(0.836 vs.\ 0.918) and the grouped CV mean is 0.760.  The right-channel result reveals
the underlying cause: the FCN requires sufficient labeled examples to learn
latent temporal structure from a 3-second context window, and the right-button
corpus (6,645 cycles) provides insufficient coverage.  On the left channel
(39,088 cycles) the FCN reaches a near-identical IoU to the RF (0.915 vs.\
0.916), suggesting the two models converge to comparable boundary estimates
once data is plentiful; however, the grouped cross-validation collapse shows this
apparent parity does not reflect true cross-participant generalisation.

\paragraph{The heuristic's IoU anomaly.}
An unexpected finding is that the heuristic's IoU on matched clicks (0.916) matches
the RF-L IoU (0.916) despite the RF's far higher deployment F1.  The heuristic
anchors its duration estimate on the OS digital event, which provides a reliable
proxy for the full biomechanical press-to-release window; when the OS event is
present and the heuristic correctly matches a ground-truth click, its Make and
Break boundaries track the label closely because both start from the same digital
anchor.  The models estimate boundaries from the analog depth signal alone, so
their per-matched-click IoU gains no ground on the heuristic even though their
overall detection rate is far superior.  This points to a natural hybrid strategy:
use the learned classifier to detect clicks the OS cannot register, and refine the
duration estimate using the OS event when it is available.

\paragraph{Failure mode analysis.}
Fold~4 consistently produces the lowest deployment F1 across the tree-based models
(RF-L: 0.910, GBT-R: 0.839).  Each grouped cross-validation fold holds out a specific
participant group, and the consistent ranking suggests the participants held out in
fold~4 share an atypical click profile --- likely shallow resting depth or
micro-tremor-rich idle signals that cause frequent false positives or missed
detections.  This is a known challenge in per-user generalization: a model trained
on other participants may not have seen enough examples of extreme profiles to learn
reliable background rejection for that subpopulation.  Active learning over the
cross-validation folds --- prioritizing annotation of participants whose profiles
diverge most from the training distribution --- is a natural direction for reducing
this variance.

\paragraph{Practical implications of the optimizer result.}
The static mid-travel default (AP~5/10) assumes the user can press comfortably to
50\,\% of travel before actuation, incurring a mean make latency of 27.0\,ms relative
to the user's biomechanical intent.  The optimized AP typically moves the threshold to
shallower travel, reducing make latency to 15.4\,ms and enabling the firmware to
register the user's intent almost immediately after the biomechanical onset.  An
11.6\,ms reduction is on the order of a perceptual just-noticeable difference for
individual input events, making this a plausibly perceptible responsiveness gain.
Setting the detection-cost parameter to $c_\text{det}=40$\,ms also eliminates
degenerate maximum-RT-hysteresis selections, so break latency is penalized
consistently across all 132 sessions without a measurable cost to TP rate.

\paragraph{Architecture and training speed.}
For deployment in a post-session recommendation workflow, training time is a practical
constraint.  The RF right-channel model trains in 141\,s on a consumer CPU, and the
left-channel model in 23 minutes.  The GBT and RF reach near-identical grouped
cross-validation generalisation (mean F1 $\approx$0.933 on the L channel), making the
RF a strong production choice given its markedly lower training latency than the FCN.
The FCN's 44-minute CPU training time is acceptable for occasional offline
re-calibration but precludes on-demand per-session retraining; GPU acceleration would
reduce this by an order of magnitude and may change the architecture trade-off in
favour of the FCN once the right-channel corpus is expanded.

\paragraph{The role of context probes.}
The dominance of context probes over engineered kinematic features
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-channel-ablation}) indicates that the production RF is
effectively operating as a probe-based trajectory pattern matcher over a
100\,ms raw-depth window, with kinematic channels providing a modest
regularisation signal on top.  This reframes the RF-versus-FCN comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-results}: both architectures ultimately draw most of their
signal from local raw-depth context rather than from explicitly derived
features, so the FCN's much larger receptive field is not yet paying for itself
at current dataset sizes.
