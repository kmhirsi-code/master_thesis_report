\chapter{Conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\section{Summary}

This thesis investigated whether per-user variation in analog mouse-click telemetry is
structured enough to support automatic labeling, statistical modeling, and firmware
parameter optimization beyond static default settings.  The answer is affirmative on
all three fronts.

\paragraph{Labeling pipeline.}
A hybrid annotation pipeline was designed and deployed at scale across two public
gaming events (BIC and PolyLAN), producing a corpus of 132 annotated recordings from
39 participants totaling 3.4 hours of verified 1\,kHz telemetry with 39,088 left-button
and 6,645 right-button click cycles.  The pipeline combines an OS-event-anchored
walk-back heuristic for bootstrapping with model-assisted labeling and human review,
reducing per-recording annotation burden while maintaining biomechanical fidelity.

\paragraph{Click-intent classification.}
Three architectures were trained and evaluated on a held-out participant-level test
set.  The Random Forest achieves a deployment F1 of 0.967 on the left channel (LOPO
mean 0.921 $\pm$ 0.024), with make- and break-boundary bias under 1.5\,ms and timing
spread under 2.2\,ms --- all well within the perceptual threshold for click timing.
The Gradient Boosted Trees model matches this generalization performance while offering
slightly higher recall, at the cost of $8\times$ longer training time.  The dilated
residual FCN provides no measurable advantage on the left channel but degrades
substantially on the right channel (F1 0.813) due to data scarcity, confirming that its
3\,s receptive field benefits require a larger corpus than currently available.  All
three models improve substantially over the heuristic baseline (deployment F1 0.373 on
the left channel), directly validating the value of the learned pipeline.

\paragraph{Firmware threshold optimizer.}
The FSM optimizer was evaluated by comparing the per-user optimized (AP, RT)
configuration against the device's static mid-travel default across 238 channel-records
from 39 participants.  The optimizer reduces optimizer loss by 46\,\% (mean), increases
the TP rate from 90.1\,\% to 95.0\,\%, and --- most importantly --- reduces mean make
latency from 31.2\,ms to 14.4\,ms, a 16.8\,ms improvement that exceeds the
per-keystroke perceptual threshold.  This demonstrates that per-user calibration
produces a directly perceptible responsiveness gain over factory defaults.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion-lim}

Several limitations constrain the current scope.  First, the right-button corpus is
approximately six times smaller than the left, causing both the FCN and the heuristic
to degrade significantly on that channel; a balanced collection protocol is needed for
production right-channel deployment.  Second, the optimizer's detection-cost parameter
($c_\text{det}=2.5$\,ms) leads to degenerate RT configurations in 4\,\% of sessions,
trading break-timing accuracy for higher TP rate; this sensitivity should be resolved
before user-facing deployment.  Third, the evaluation relies on a single hardware
platform (HITS-enabled PRO X2 Superstrike); generalization to other analog input
devices with different sensor resolutions or firmware architectures has not been
assessed.  Finally, ablation studies for the channel-derivative pipeline and the GBT
asymmetric soft-label design were not completed within the scope of this thesis and
remain as open verification items.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-fw}

\paragraph{On-device inference.}
The current pipeline is offline.  Quantizing the Random Forest to INT8 and deploying
it on the peripheral's MCU would enable real-time per-frame classification at 1\,kHz
without a host connection, matching the latency requirements identified in the
Background chapter.

\paragraph{Adaptive recalibration.}
Neuromotor fatigue shifts the analog depth signal over the course of a gaming session.
A lightweight online recalibration loop --- monitoring the running mean of the resting
drift channel and nudging the actuation threshold accordingly --- could maintain the
optimized configuration as the user's biomechanical state changes.

\paragraph{Cross-device generalization.}
The labeling and modeling pipeline is device-agnostic in principle; replicating the
data collection on Hall-effect keyboards and alternative mouse switches would test
whether the trained feature representations transfer, and whether a single universal
model is feasible or per-device adaptation is required.

\paragraph{Ablation and soft-label validation.}
Completing the channel-ablation and GBT soft-label ablation experiments would
quantify the individual contribution of each engineered feature group and confirm
that the asymmetric split-normal Gaussian targets measurably outperform symmetric
or hard-label alternatives.

\paragraph{User perception study.}
An end-to-end perceptual study --- presenting participants with their static-default
and per-user-optimized configurations in counterbalanced order and measuring
self-reported latency and accuracy --- would close the loop between the objective
timing improvements reported here and the subjective experience that motivates the
work.
